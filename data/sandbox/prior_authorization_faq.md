## About this FAQ

A prior authorization is a review Harbor completes before it covers certain medicines. Members often call when the pharmacy tells them a medicine "needs approval". This FAQ gives advocates the answers to the questions members and prescriber offices ask most often. If a question is not here, use the advocate desktop search, and if search has no approved answer, flag the call so Knowledge Ops can follow up.

## Starting a request

**Who can start a prior authorization?**

Usually the prescriber office starts it. A member can call to ask Harbor to start one when the office has already sent the prescriber request.

**How do I start one on a call?**

Start a prior authorization by entering the prescriber request in the intake tool before you promise a date.

If the intake tool shows no prescriber request, tell the member that Harbor needs the request from the prescriber office first. Offer to send the office a fax reminder from the desktop.

**What needs to be attached?**

Attach the prescriber notes and the member medicine list to the request.

Missing notes are the most common reason a request is pended. Check the attachments panel before you submit.

## Timing

**How long does a decision take?**

A standard prior authorization decision takes 4 business days after Harbor receives a complete request. An expedited request takes 24 hours. These figures are illustrative.

**When is a request expedited?**

A request is expedited when the prescriber says that waiting for a standard decision could seriously harm the member's health. Only the prescriber can ask for an expedited review.

**Can I check the status?**

Yes. The status panel in the intake tool shows Received, In review, Decided, or Pended for information.

## After the decision

**Who hears about the decision?**

Tell the member that the prescriber office receives the decision, and offer to explain the next step in plain words.

The member also receives a letter. If the request is denied, the letter explains how to appeal.

**What if the member disagrees with a denial?**

Explain that they or their prescriber can ask for a redetermination. Transfer the call to the appeals line. Do not predict the outcome of an appeal.

## Clinical boundaries

Do not advise the member to stop a prescribed medicine while a prior authorization is pending.

If the member asks whether they can take something else in the meantime, tell them to ask their prescriber or pharmacist, and offer to transfer them to the pharmacist line.
