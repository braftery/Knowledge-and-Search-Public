## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how a Harbor pharmacy advocate opens a specialty pharmacy intake when a member or a prescriber office calls about a specialty medicine. Specialty medicines include injectable, infused, and high-cost oral medicines that need special handling, monitoring, or storage. The goal is a complete intake on the first call, so the specialty desk can start benefit checks without calling the member back.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to pharmacy advocates in the Harbor contact center who take calls for Harbor Medicare Advantage members. It does not cover infusion scheduling, home health nursing, or appeals of a coverage decision. Those calls are transferred using the routing table in the advocate desktop.

## 3. Roles

- Pharmacy advocate: confirms eligibility, collects intake details, and hands the packet to the specialty desk.
- Specialty desk technician: checks benefits, contacts the prescriber office, and schedules the first fill.
- Specialty pharmacist: reviews clinical questions. Advocates never answer clinical questions about a specialty medicine.
- Team lead: approves exceptions to this procedure and reviews intakes that miss the same-day handoff.

## 4. Before you start

Verify the caller with the standard two-factor check before you discuss any medicine. If the caller is a prescriber office, confirm the office name and the National Provider Identifier on the request. If the caller is a caregiver, confirm that an authorized representative form is on file.

## 5. Intake steps

Confirm the specialty referral is on the account before you open an intake.

If no referral is on file, tell the caller that the prescriber office needs to send one, and give them the specialty fax number from the desktop. Do not open an intake without a referral, because the specialty desk will reject it.

Record the prescriber name and the medicine name on the intake form before you save it.

Also record the strength, the dosing schedule as written by the prescriber, the diagnosis code if the office provides it, and the date the member last received the medicine. Leave a field blank rather than guess.

Ask the member where they want the medicine delivered, and whether someone will be home to sign for a refrigerated package. Note any delivery limits in the delivery field.

Send the completed intake packet to the specialty desk on the same business day.

Use the Send to specialty button in the desktop. The packet goes to the specialty work queue with a time stamp. If the call ends after 4:00 p.m. Eastern, the packet still goes the same day and is worked the next morning. These hours are illustrative.

## 6. Cost

The specialty intake copay listed for this step is $40. This figure is illustrative.

The copay a member pays for a specialty medicine depends on their plan, their stage of coverage, and the medicine tier. Quote cost only with the approved pricing disclaimer, and only after the benefit check is complete.

## 7. Transfers and mail order

Members sometimes ask to move a specialty medicine to the mail-order pharmacy to save time or money.

Specialty drugs stay at Harbor Specialty Pharmacy and are not converted to mail order on this call.

If the member wants to discuss a different pharmacy, create a follow-up task for the specialty desk and tell the member to expect a call within two business days. These timeframes are illustrative.

## 8. Exceptions and escalation

- Urgent need: if the prescriber office says the member will run out within three days, mark the intake urgent and call the specialty desk line directly.
- Clinical questions: transfer to the specialty pharmacist. Do not interpret dosing, side effects, or interactions.
- Complaints: log a grievance in the desktop using the grievance reason codes.

## 9. Records

Intake packets are kept in the specialty work queue for seven years under the Harbor records schedule. Do not copy intake details into personal notes or email.
