## About this FAQ

This FAQ is the page pharmacy advocates and members use when a pharmacy says a medicine needs approval before it can be filled. It collects the answers advocates give most often on the Harbor Medicare Advantage pharmacy line. Pharmacy Knowledge keeps it next to the specialty intake SOP so the two pages stay easy to tell apart: the SOP opens a specialty case, and this FAQ talks about the approval that sometimes sits in front of a fill.

The FAQ applies to prior authorization questions on medicines Harbor's pharmacy benefit covers for Medicare Advantage members. It does not decide medical-benefit drugs, it does not set visit costs, and it does not replace the member's plan document when that document prints a fee or a filing window this page leaves out. Calls from prescriber offices are in scope when they reach the pharmacy queue.

Harbor added the page after advocates were writing different dates on the same kind of request, depending on which team lead had last updated a sticky note. The notes disagreed, and members were hearing more than one clock for a standard decision. Pharmacy Knowledge retired the notes and put the clock in this FAQ. The page is reviewed when the appeals desk changes a letter, and the last-updated note at the bottom is the record of that review.

In this FAQ, prior authorization means a review of a prescribed medicine before the pharmacy fills it.

## Starting a request

Advocates open this section when the member or the prescriber office is trying to get a request into the intake tool.

Start a prior authorization only after you enter the prescriber's written request in the intake tool, and do not promise the member a decision date before that entry is saved.

Attach the prescriber's clinical notes and the member's current medicine list before you submit the request.

Only the prescriber may open a prior authorization request.

Can I ask about the status of a prior authorization? Yes. Call the number on your member ID card and ask the advocate to read the status on the request.

When the prescriber signature is missing, hold the file at the desk and do not escalate to the specialty supervisor.

## Timing

Timing questions are the ones members ask before they hang up. This section gives the standard clock. Other clocks are named here only so the advocate knows which page does not hold them.

A standard prior authorization decision is due 7 calendar days after Harbor receives a complete request. This figure is illustrative.

Tell the member that the decision letter goes to the prescriber office, and offer to explain that letter in plain words.

The number of days a prescriber has to schedule a peer-to-peer review after a denial is in the clinical operations memo. This FAQ does not state that number of days.

## After the decision

Once the case shows a decision, advocates use this section for the letter, the portal note, and the example cost.

A summary of the prior authorization decision appears in the member portal within 3 business days. This figure is illustrative.

The illustrative specialty intake copay at a preferred network pharmacy is $65.

## Appeals

Appeals questions start when the member disagrees with the decision letter. This section covers who may ask and how the desk records the ask. Fees and later review steps live on the plan document.

An appeal may be filed up to 60 calendar days after the date on the decision letter. This figure is illustrative.

Record the appeal in the case file on the same day the member asks for it, and send the file to the appeals desk.

Who can ask for an appeal? The member may ask, and so may a caregiver with an authorization on the account.

Any fee charged to file an appeal is printed on the member's plan document. This FAQ does not state a fee.

## Clinical boundaries

Clinical questions come up on the same calls as status questions. This section is the boundary language.

Read the next block word for word when a member asks if the medicine is right for them.

Verbatim (Legal): I cannot tell you whether this medicine is right for you. Your prescriber makes that decision. I can tell you the status of the request on your plan record today.

Do not tell the member to stop, start, or change a prescribed medicine while a prior authorization is pending.

Do not recommend a dose, a substitute medicine, or a days' supply. Send clinical questions back to the prescriber.
